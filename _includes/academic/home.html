Research

Working Papers

Green Lands, Green Games: Monitoring and the Allocation of Public Investment

Independent research · September 2026

Abstract

Third-party supervision that curbs data falsification can increase the supply of public goods. I show how it can nevertheless distort their allocation. I study China’s 2016 transfer of air-quality monitoring to third-party operators, using a multitask Tullock model to examine its effects on green-space allocation. Difference-in-differences estimates show that parks’ share of supplied land area rises by 1.83 percentage points within 5 kilometers of monitors relative to 20–50 kilometers, accompanied by a relative decline of 0.703 micrograms per cubic meter in satellite-derived PM2.5. Effects are larger where pre-reform reports suggest more underreporting, among younger officials, and in cities with pollution-reduction targets. Counterfactual analysis shows that reallocating each year’s park land, without changing its total area, raises residents’ average green-space benefits. Thus, even when third-party supervision increases the supply of public goods, it may distort their allocation.
